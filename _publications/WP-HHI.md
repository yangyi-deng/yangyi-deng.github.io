We develop a welfare-based concentration index that generalizes the Herfindahl--Hirschman Index (HHI) by incorporating pairwise product interactions. In differentiated-product markets, it adjusts the HHI for the strength and direction of product interactions, raising measured concentration for substitutes and lowering it for complements. Combining the index with aggregate output yields welfare indices whose interaction weights reflect the relative emphasis on consumers versus producers. Merger welfare effects therefore depend jointly on the levels of and changes in concentration and aggregate output. An application to U.S. automobiles illustrates how product interactions affect the assessment of concentration and welfare.
